# Style Patterns

## Purpose

Combine professional rigor, figure-led teaching, and restrained WeChat visual rhythm. Use patterns, not another publisher's branding or wording.

## Recommended Voice

- Curious, precise, and explanatory.
- Translate jargon into a concrete mechanism, then restore the technical term.
- Prefer causal explanation over adjectives.
- Write for an intelligent reader who has not read the paper.

## Visual Rhythm

- Body: 14-16px, line height around 1.8.
- Paragraphs: usually 40-120 Chinese characters; one idea each.
- H2: strong section boundary; use a restrained accent.
- H3: local question or mechanism; visually lighter than H2.
- Strong emphasis: 1-3 short phrases per screen, not whole paragraphs.
- Figures: one every 300-700 characters when the paper supports it.
- Captions: explain why the figure matters, not merely “Figure N”.
- Lists: use for parallel items, not ordinary prose.
- Quotes/cards: reserve for the reader promise, a key definition, or a caution.

## Style Models

### Professional Community

Use for expert audiences. Lead with the research question and preserve technical details, baselines, and limitations.

### Figure-Led Teaching

Use for architecture, agent, and system papers. Alternate explanation and meaningful figures. Explain every figure immediately after it.

### News-Led Opening

Use sparingly for broad audiences. Start with relevance or a surprising result, then quickly narrow to evidence. Avoid hype.

## SmearglePaper Default

- Purple accent for headings, emphasis, and quote cards.
- Short dense paragraphs.
- Rounded paper figures with subtle shadows.
- H2 uses a left accent bar.
- H3 uses a lighter underline.
- Captions use centered muted text.

## Avoid

- More than two decorative colors.
- Repeating “重要、强大、突破” without evidence.
- Images without explanatory text.
- Huge unbroken paragraphs.
- A long generic AI-history introduction.
- Treating later industry adoption as evidence from the original paper.
